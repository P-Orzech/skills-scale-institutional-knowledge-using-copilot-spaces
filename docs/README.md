# OctoAcme Program & Process Docs

This `docs/` folder is the single source of truth for OctoAcme's project management processes and serves as the entry point for this Copilot Space. It captures our standard workflows, roles, communication patterns, and quality practices so teams can execute work consistently, onboard faster, and improve continuously.

## Overview of OctoAcme Project Management Processes

OctoAcme's project management processes emphasize structured yet adaptable workflows to ensure consistent, high-quality outcomes across teams. Projects progress through a clear lifecycle—beginning with **Initiation**, followed by **Planning**, **Execution & Tracking**, **Release & Deployment**, and finally **Retrospective & Continuous Improvement**. During initiation, the team defines the business need, success metrics, and key stakeholders using artifacts such as a project one-pager and stakeholder communication plans. Planning activities involve breaking down work into actionable backlog items with acceptance criteria and estimates, clearly documenting risks, dependencies, and the Definition of Done (DoD), as well as mapping release timelines and milestones.

Core personas include the Project Manager (PM), Product Manager (PdM), Developers, QA/Testers, and Stakeholders, each with defined roles and responsibilities. The PM coordinates schedules, risks, and cross-team communication; the PdM sets the product vision, prioritizes backlogs, and measures outcomes; while Developers and QA focus on delivering robust, maintainable code that meets acceptance criteria. These roles work together through daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, and retrospectives to maintain alignment and drive projects forward.

Communication strategies are comprehensive and multi-layered. The team holds regular cadence meetings: daily standups for quick blockers and status checks, weekly syncs between PM and PdM, bi-weekly standups for the delivery team, and monthly stakeholder updates. Status reporting uses standardized templates for progress, risks, blockers, and decisions needed, ensuring all parties are informed and engaged throughout the project lifecycle. Escalation paths are clearly defined—from team-level triage up to sponsor-level involvement for major blockers or incidents—enabling timely resolution of issues.

Quality assurance is embedded at every stage of the process. OctoAcme requires unit and integration tests for new code, end-to-end smoke testing for critical flows, and security scanning in continuous integration (CI). All pull requests undergo reviews, testing, and require approvals before merging. Checklists for planning, execution, and deployment ensure nothing is missed, while retrospectives and a culture of continuous improvement help the team learn from each cycle and update processes to align with best practices and stakeholder feedback.
